# Design context: Jev MBTI

Status: The user chose the notebook with red-pen 첨삭 direction. The [design brief](./docs/design-brief.md) records the full proposal, and its screens on Doop canvas `l2R2l7AW3M` await approval.

## Users

Public, anonymous visitors who enjoy Korean MBTI culture, writing in Korean or English. They ask playful questions ("which type cries first at a movie?") and share the resulting charts on KakaoTalk, Instagram, and X. Mobile and desktop are equally first-class.

## Brand personality

Playful, clever, and a little cheeky, like a classroom in-joke. The site is confident about its stereotypes and honest that they are memes, not psychology. The interface should feel like a real stationery object, not an AI dashboard or a chatbot.

## Aesthetic direction

A grid-paper notebook page. Jev sticks the 16 types on as die-cut stickers, and the LLM review marks the page in a teacher's red pen with circles, arrows, margin notes, a grade, and 총평. Colors come from the desk: cool white paper, a pale blue grid, navy ballpoint ink, pencil graphite, red pen, and lavender, mint, sky, and butter stickers for NT, NF, SJ, and SP. Type is set in Pretendard for text, Bagel Fat One for sticker codes, and Nanum Pen Script for red-pen writing. Components are Fluid Functionalism, re-themed. Every typeface must include Hangul.

Jev makes structured judgments and writes nothing. Every sentence on a chart belongs to the review, which the LLM writes. Jev's original placement must stay visible whenever the review moves a type.

## Design principles

- Red ink means the review and nothing else. Technical problems use the orange warning tone and never red.
- The chart is the hero. Controls stay compact and secondary.
- Corrections are readable without color: a dashed outline, an arrow, a numbered note, and words in the list.
- Stickers never overlap, and long questions always wrap fully.
- The layout reflows for mobile rather than shrinking. A one-axis chart turns into a vertical ladder.
- Motion shows the two-step story: fast sticker drops, then deliberate pen strokes. Under reduced motion, everything appears at its final state.
